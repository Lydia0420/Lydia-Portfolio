Food & the City / Final project

What Are We Really Waiting For?

An editorial study of how social media, queues, craft, and shared memory turn a restaurant visit into an anticipated cultural experience.

Format Editorial zine
Tool Adobe InDesign
Year 2026

Before the first bite, the story has already begun. This project follows a Din Tai Fung visit from the moment it is imagined online to the moment it is remembered and shared. I used the zine to bring field notes, menu analysis, reviews, and visual research into the same sequence, so the argument unfolds much like the meal itself.

02 / Thinking through a publication

Observations, given a rhythm.

Why a zine?

The format places reviews, photographs, and text in conversation. A dense field of social messages gives way to a quieter space for interpretation.

A recurring visual language

Red headlines, warm paper tones, and large food images recur throughout the publication. Changes in density carry the reader from online anticipation to the memory of a meal.

03 / Unpack a meal

More than what is served.

Unfold four layers from the original zine to explore how media, craft, and memory construct an urban dining experience.

04 / From course to publication

A course question, made tangible.

Media shapes practice

The course asks how media does more than describe food: it also guides practices around it. Reservations, photography, and queues become ways to examine how dining is imagined before arrival.

Food connects identity and memory

The project expands its focus from a dumpling to the people who wait, watch, and share. Food becomes a way to consider relationships with other people and with the city.

Observation becomes an argument

The final zine sequences reviews, brand imagery, menu analysis, and field observations into a reading experience. It offers an interpretation to consider, rather than a universal account of every customer.
