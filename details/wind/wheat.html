01 / THE QUESTION

How do you draw
something you
cannot see?

I began with wind: invisible on its own, but unmistakable in what it moves. Instead of drawing the force, I built a field of wheat to reveal its presence.

Each blade grows, bends, sways and catches light. Together they form a landscape shaped by shared forces, without losing the small differences that make it feel alive.

The challenge: moving together,
without moving exactly alike.
A SMALL ENCOUNTER WITH WIND

A little movement.
A shared response.

Brush across the stems. Watch a gesture travel through the cluster, then soften as each blade finds its way back.

Move to stir the field.

03 / BUILDING A LIVING SYSTEM

One blade.
A thousand small differences.

From one blade to a living field.

A blade with its
own character.

A Wheat class gives every blade its own height, color, position, growth speed and motion phase. The first step was a single object that could grow and change, rather than a static mark.

04 / THE SURFACE & THE SYSTEM

A landscape to feel.
A structure to understand.

The environment has a voice.

I created and edited the soundscape in REAPER, then connected it to the wind system. Sound supports the movement and atmosphere, rather than sitting apart from the field.

The logic becomes visible.

The wireframe exposes the curves, vectors and control points beneath the surface. What began as a technical view became another way to see the work.

05 / WHAT THE FIELD TAUGHT ME

Natural feeling.
Deliberate decisions.

Constraints shaped the visual language.

Floating letters as wheat spikes were too expensive to animate. Replacing them with line-based geometry made the sketch more playable while preserving its character.

Controls became part of making.

Adjusting parameters in real time let me test ideas without restarting the sketch. The interface became a way to understand the relationship between code, motion and feeling.

Sound opens another direction.

A closer relationship between movement and audio could make the environment feel more alive. Body movement or environmental input is a possible next step, not a feature of the current work.

Peer feedback & next refinements

The original critique highlighted the poetic concept, visible technical progression and combination of visuals, controls and sound. It also called for clearer interaction guidance, simpler explanations of technical terms and a motion preview. This page brings the first two points forward; the full movement remains in the live sketch.
